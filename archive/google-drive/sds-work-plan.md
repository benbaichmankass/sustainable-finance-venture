> **Status: predecessor thinking, retained for lineage.** This is the infrastructure/energy-project framing that preceded the community-finance thesis. Its structural ideas — pooling projects with uncorrelated idiosyncratic risk, designing projects to capture induced growth, public-market distribution — carry directly into the current securitization design and are reflected in `product-design/business-plan.md`.

---

## 1. Goals

1. To fill the $125T funding gap for clean-energy transition, especially in the global south
2. To fill the financing gap for infrastructure in the developing world
3. To advance environmentally and socially sustainable and equitable development

## 2. Areas where investment is needed

1. **Transit**
   1. R&D
   2. Electrification
   3. Battery Tech
   4. Alt fuels — tech and supply chains
2. **Energy**
   1. Meeting new capacity needs w/o fossil fuels
   2. Replacing high-emission sources of capacity on the grid
   3. Transmission infrastructure
   4. Energy Storage
   5. 'Smart' consumption
   6. Development of open, competitive and reliable energy markets
3. **Construction**
4. **Industrial Processes**

## 3. Why is there an infrastructure funding gap? — Process Analysis

1. **Planning**
   1. Are there enough project proposals?
   2. Can the project be delivered within budgetary and time constraints?
   3. Does the project make a competitive value proposition?
   4. Does the project create value for all stakeholders? This includes trying to help losers at least 'break even'
   5. Lean towards predictability
      1. Using repetitive and iterative design to embrace proven concepts
      2. Build quickly
      3. Capture economies-of-scale benefits
      4. Design modularly, arranging pre-designed units in ways that optimize project value proposition
2. **Decision Making** *(not filled in)*
3. **Execution**
4. **Performance**

## 4. Optimizing project design to optimize financing

1. **Project structure**
   1. Design projects to include elements that capture some gains of economic growth induced by the core project (e.g. business loans to businesses that can grow from increased access to electricity — such as loans to farmers for equipment that requires electricity which they previously lacked, refrigerated agricultural collection facilities, loans for trucks where new roads are built, real estate developments close to infrastructure projects).

      > 'To scale private finance globally, public finance should be deployed to lower the cost of capital for private investors who, due to the respective risks, require a rate of return that is three to ten times higher in developing economies than in the EU or the U.S. … Public financial institutions should channel their funding to mobilize private finance and achieve higher impact, including by providing political risk support, guarantees to reduce foreign exchange risk, and liquidity to increase funding for less commercially viable sectors and regions.'

   2. Employing storage for arbitrage to maximize revenue from generation
   3. Business/Development loans with future cash flows from electric generation/storage/arbitrage as collateral
2. **Using emerging but proven technologies to optimize and future-proof project performance**
   1. Wireless energy transfer for supergrid? Microwaves and satellites?
3. **'Product-Value Optimization'**
   1. Encouraging usage of assets for highest value-creation applications (pushing capacity towards productive uses, not just consumption)
   2. Watt-bit spread

## 5. Capital Structure

1. Ownership shares in exchange for capital resources (not just finance)
2. Timeline — differentiated for separate parts of loans, allowing for optimal payments based on expected cash flow over time
3. Have as much risk insured as possible (or using swaps/derivatives) to minimize downsides

## 6. Risk reduction through verticalization

1. Designing simple, repeatable projects (e.g. microgrids)
2. Domain expertise in project management in certain markets/regions — 'streamlining' of risk management in inaccessible markets
3. Collective insurance scheme, reinsurance to distribute risk, total liability to assets ratios, risk swapping

## 7. Optimizing securitized assets

1. Multi-faceted projects that can capture growth generated by projects
2. Different blends of equity/debt/derivatives/hybrids for different product needs
3. Different risk profiles to create different risk/reward structures
   1. Combining different types of projects with different risk profiles to neutralize idiosyncratic risks:
      1. Project timeline
      2. Project type
      3. Country/Region
4. There's more capital to be raised in public markets than private equity (though institutional and private equity investors/partners are also important). The split of global equity between private and public markets by valuation is roughly **85-90% public** and **10-15% private**.
5. What kinds of public securities are there for infrastructure projects today? E.g. project/municipal bonds, non-taxed interest profit loans
6. What is the potential, in dollars, for publicly traded project securities?
7. Lower management fees for longer-committed capital

## 8. Research

1. Industry/Comp overview
2. Prices and models
3. How to create and market a publicly-traded security
   1. How to keep things as simple as possible from a regulatory standpoint
4. Understand how to manage risk within a security
   1. Transparency, so risk assessment can be externally validated
5. Create different risk-profiles with specific risk/reward scores so as to have a guide for structuring different classes of securities
   1. What is an ideal (or maximum viable) amount of risk for different kinds of investors?
   2. What is the ideal (or minimum viable) profitability for different kinds of investors? What amount of risk is acceptable?
