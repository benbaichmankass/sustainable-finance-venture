# Track C — Practice First, Structurer Later

**Status:** Detail for a decision not yet taken (OQ-19) · **Owner:** BB · **Drafted:** 2026-09-08

`docs/venture/solo-operator-track.md` set out three operating models and recommended **(c)** —
run the solo design-and-arranging practice now, convert to the capital-markets structurer at
deal 3–4. This document is the detail behind that recommendation, written so the option can be
judged rather than taken on trust. **Nothing here is decided.** OQ-19 remains Open.

---

## 1. What makes this a plan and not a fudge

"Do the small thing first and the big thing later" is what every deferred decision sounds like.
Track C is only a real plan if four things are specified, and this document exists to specify them:

| | The question a fudge cannot answer |
| :---- | :---- |
| **§4 Gates** | What exactly has to be true before you convert? Named, measurable, decidable by evidence rather than appetite. |
| **§5 Carry-forward** | What must Phase 1 build *only because* Phase 3 needs it? Without this list, Phase 1 is a different business that happens to come first. |
| **§6 Prohibitions** | What must Phase 1 refuse, because accepting it forecloses Phase 3? |
| **§8 Terminal state** | Is stopping at Phase 2 a failure or a legitimate outcome? If it is a failure, Phase 1 gets under-invested and the plan eats itself. |

The single most consequential item in the whole document is **§5.1, data rights**. It is one
clause in an engagement letter, it costs nothing to insert in month 3, and it cannot be
retrofitted in year 3.

## 2. The three phases

| | **Phase 1 — Practice** | **Phase 2 — Arranger** | **Phase 3 — Structurer** |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Months** | 0–18 | 12–36 | 30–60 |
| **What you are** | A design and diagnostics practice | The party that assembles facilities | The sponsor of a pooled structure |
| **What you sell** | Readiness diagnostics; funder-paid design mandates | Retainer plus success fee on closed facilities | Structuring fee, servicing bps, retained interest |
| **Revenue lanes** (`solo-operator-track.md` §3.2) | A, B | B, C | C, D |
| **Capital needed** | Working capital only | Working capital only | **Retention strip — the model stops being capital-light here** |
| **Headcount** | You, plus subcontractors | You, plus subcontractors | First hires become unavoidable |
| **Edge theories live** (`business-plan.md` §5) | 7 instrumentation, 3 standard-setting | 7, 3, 6 verification | 7, 3, 6, **5 tranching precision**, 2 via partner |
| **Regulatory footing** | Advisory, unlicensed (OQ-20) | Advisory or co-mandate with a licensed arranger | Licensed entity or a licensed partner, unavoidably |
| **Deals** | none | Deal 1 bilateral guaranteed; deal 2 club | Deal 3 warehouse; deal 4 first pooled structure |

The phases **overlap deliberately**. Phase 2 starts while Phase 1 is still the income; Phase 3
preparation starts while Phase 2 is still the income. At no point is there a quarter with no
revenue because you are "transitioning".

## 3. What happens in each phase

### Phase 1 — Practice (months 0–18)

Delivery is `solo-operator-track.md` §8 and milestones M-36…M-40. In summary:

- Months 0–1: OQ-20 answered with counsel; market chosen on commercial criteria; the offer
  written as a signable scope of work; entity, PI insurance, engagement template.
- Months 1–2: outreach wave 1 — standards bodies, then originators led with EXP-22, research
  institutions in parallel, capital and risk carriers last.
- Month 3: first paid diagnostic closed. **This is the quarter's only hard success criterion.**
- Months 4–9: deliver engagement 1; produce the ranked per-field cost curve; RT-1 reaches v1.0
  and is **published openly**; EXP-25 data access negotiated with the research partner.
- Months 9–18: engagements 2 and 3; design of the first facility begins.

**Phase 1 is finished when:** three diagnostics are delivered, RT-1 v1.0 is published, one
research institution has signed, and a facility is in design with an identified investor.

### Phase 2 — Arranger (months 12–36)

- **Deal 1** — a guarantee-wrapped bilateral facility (`solo-operator-track.md` §4): one investor,
  one originator you already diagnosed, portfolio originated to RT-1, monitored with RT-3,
  concessional first-loss sized against RT-5. No SPV, no rating, no true-sale opinion, no
  minimum pool size. Target: term sheet month 12–15, close month 18–24.
- **Deal 2** — the same structure with two or three originators sharing a jurisdiction and one
  legal opinion. This is the first structure that tests the multi-originator question, which is
  why **OQ-18 must be settled before deal 2 is assembled, not after.**
- Alongside: EXP-25 analysis completes and the working paper is drafted; engagements 4–6 run;
  Lane A becomes recurring rather than episodic.

**Phase 2 is finished when:** two facilities are closed and performing, both originating to RT-1
from day one, and the EXP-25 estimate exists with a confidence interval.

### Phase 3 — Structurer (months 30–60)

- **Deal 3** — a revolving warehouse, which is the first structure that needs an SPV and therefore
  the first that carries the fixed-cost stack RT-6 models. RT-6's own extension list already
  flags that a revolving warehouse recycling principal several times a year is the right model for
  short-tenor community assets, and that the current single-life treatment understates the fee
  base (M-25).
- **Deal 4** — the first pooled structure with a rated or privately-placed senior tranche. This is
  where theory 5 (tranching precision) finally monetises: the EXP-25 correlation estimate sizes
  first-loss, and every basis point saved on the junior layer is senior capital that clears cheaper.
- The venture at this point is the business in `product-design/business-plan.md`, reached with a
  track record instead of on a promise.

## 4. The conversion gates

**Seven conditions.** Convert only when all seven hold. Each is checkable against evidence the
plan already generates, and five of them are things Phase 1 and 2 produce as a by-product.

| # | Gate | Why this threshold | Where the evidence comes from |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **1** | **A contractible pipeline of ≥ USD 20m** across originators already diagnosed | RT-5's sweep: fixed costs dominate below ~USD 5m and flatten around USD 20–40m. Below the flattening point the SPV eats the economics. | RT-5 `--sweep`, refreshed with real numbers |
| **2** | **≥ 24 months of clean repayment data**, originated to RT-1 from day one | LIT-004 and LIT-006: 2–3 years of clean repayment history before a first tranche | The Phase 2 facilities — see §5.2 |
| **3** | **EXP-25 has returned a market-level correlation with a usable interval** | `business-plan.md` §5 names this as the killer of theory 5: "an interval so wide that no defensible first-loss saving exists". A wide interval is a legitimate no. | EXP-25, with the research partner |
| **4** | **OQ-18 settled** — multi-originator diversifies on the right axis | LIT-036/LIT-037: the correlation channel that predicts trouble is market-level, so a regional multi-originator pool may concentrate rather than spread risk. If it does, the pool has to be multi-market, which is slower and more expensive. | EXP-25's crossed design |
| **5** | **OQ-1 closed with counsel** — a true-sale opinion is obtainable in the chosen jurisdiction | LIT-009's six-point checklist. `business-plan.md` §9 lists cross-border true-sale failure as a legal risk to close **before** committing to a geography. | Counsel (PT-09) |
| **6** | **The retention capital is identified and committed** | Sponsoring a securitisation placed with EU/UK investors requires retaining a minimum economic interest (MEMO-3). **MEMO-3 does not record the percentage** — get it from counsel and size the ask before Phase 3, not during. This is the line where the business stops being capital-light. | Counsel (PT-09), then RT-6 |
| **7** | **An anchor investor is secured before structuring begins** | `business-plan.md` §9's own mitigation for the market risk: no appetite for a novel asset class at pilot scale. Structuring on spec is the expensive way to discover this. | Phase 2 relationships |

**Gates 3 and 4 can return a permanent no.** If the correlation interval is too wide, or the
diversification runs the wrong way, Phase 3 as designed does not exist and the right answer is to
stay a practice (§8). That is not a hedge — it is the falsification test `business-plan.md` §5
already committed to, arriving at the moment it can actually change a decision.

## 5. Carry-forward — what Phase 1 must build for Phase 3

This is what makes C one business rather than two. Each item is cheap now and impossible later.

### 5.1 Data rights — the clause that matters most

**Every engagement letter and every facility agreement must grant retained, ongoing rights to
pseudonymised loan-level data for portfolio analytics and structuring — not use limited to that
single engagement.**

`business-plan.md` §5 already names the falsifier of theory 7 as "originators adopt the protocol
and then decline to grant data rights". That failure does not happen at the moment of refusal; it
happens at the moment you sign an engagement letter that is silent on the point, and it is
discovered two years later when the data you assumed you had is not yours to pool.

Three properties the clause needs:

1. **Retained and perpetual for the data generated during the engagement**, not revocable on
   termination.
2. **Purpose-scoped widely enough to cover pooling and third-party structuring**, since a purpose
   limited to "advising the client" excludes exactly the Phase 3 use.
3. **Pseudonymised at source, with no personal identifiers requested** — which is both the privacy
   posture this project already takes (CLAUDE.md §8, and the EXP-25 ask) and the thing that makes
   a data-protection officer say yes rather than escalate.

Draft it once, with counsel, alongside the OQ-20 perimeter question. It goes in the engagement
template in month 1 (M-37), before the first client, and never gets negotiated ad hoc under time
pressure.

### 5.2 The seasoning clock

Gate 2 needs 24 months of clean repayment data. **The clock starts the day deal 1's first loan is
originated to RT-1, not the day you decide to convert.** Deal 1 closing at month 18 means the data
matures at month 42.

Two consequences, and they are the strongest arguments in this document for moving early:

- **Close deal 1 early even if it is small.** A USD 2m facility that starts the clock is worth more
  to Phase 3 than a USD 10m facility eighteen months later. Size is a Phase 3 gate; *seasoning is a
  Phase 1 decision*.
- **Every Phase 1 diagnostic should end with the originator capturing RT-1 fields**, whether or not
  a facility follows. Data that seasons from month 6 is the cheapest asset in the plan.

### 5.3 Right of first refusal on refinancing and scale-up

Phase 3's first pool will most likely be built out of Phase 2's facilities. Put a right of first
refusal on refinancing, upsizing and any successor facility into deal 1 and deal 2. It costs the
counterparty nothing at signing and it is the difference between a warehouse you can assemble and
one you have to re-win against whoever the originator meets in year three.

### 5.4 The published standard

RT-1 v1.0 published openly, early. Adoption is the moat for theory 3, and `business-plan.md` §5
names the falsifier as "a DFI, network body or ratings agency publishes a competing standard
first; adoption is winner-take-most". Adoption compounds and cannot be accelerated later by
spending money. This is the one Phase 1 deliverable whose value is destroyed by delay rather than
merely postponed.

### 5.5 The correlation pipeline, not the number

`business-plan.md` §5 is explicit: the moat is "the pipeline that keeps producing it as markets
shift, and the partner relationships that supply the data — treat the estimate as a product with a
shelf life, not an asset." Phase 1's job is the pipeline: the data-rights clauses, the research
MOU, and a reproducible estimation codebase. The number is a Phase 2 output.

## 6. What Phase 1 must refuse

Five things that look like good early revenue and foreclose Phase 3:

| Refuse | Because |
| :---- | :---- |
| **A data clause limited to the engagement** | §5.1. The single irreversible mistake available in year one. |
| **Equity in an originator** | Turns an asset-light practice into an investor, and creates a conflict at exactly the moment you structure that originator's book for third-party capital. |
| **Becoming servicer of record** | Operationally heavy, needs staff, and breaks the no-hiring constraint. Supply the monitoring tooling; do not become the collections operation. |
| **Exclusivity with one originator** | Kills the multi-originator pool before OQ-18 has even been asked. |
| **A first facility structured as a securitisation** | §4 of `solo-operator-track.md` and gate 1 above. Arithmetically wrong at achievable scale, and a failed first structure is more damaging than a slow one. |

## 7. Economics — the shape, and what still has to be filled in

**RT-6 currently models the wrong entity for Phases 1 and 2.** It is a per-deal securitisation P&L
carrying an **[assumed]** ~USD 250k fixed structuring cost. In Phase 1 that cost does not arise,
and in Phase 2 it is the client's. RT-6 becomes the right model at Phase 3 and only there.

What is needed instead is a small services-P&L variant. The arithmetic is not complicated, and it
is written out here rather than fabricated into projections, because the two drivers that decide
it are yours to set, not the literature's:

**Phase 1 — practice**

```
billable_days_per_year = working_days x utilisation
annual_revenue         = effective_day_rate x billable_days_per_year + mandate_fees
break_even_days        = annual_fixed_cost / effective_day_rate
runway_months          = starting_cash / (annual_fixed_cost / 12)
```

The two drivers that decide Phase 1 are **effective day rate** and **utilisation**, and neither has
a benchmark in this repo. Utilisation in particular is where solo practices are usually wrong:
selling, writing, travelling and admin are not billable, and the gap between available days and
billable days is the business. Set both from your own judgement, mark them `ASSUMED`, and let the
break-even identity above tell you how many days you actually have to sell.

**Phase 2 — arranger.** Adds `retainer x months + success_fee_rate x facility_notional`. The
retainer is the load-bearing term: it converts an 18-month sales cycle into billable months, and
without it Phase 2 is unpaid work with a lottery ticket at the end.

**Phase 3 — structurer.** Adds structuring fee, servicing basis points on assets under management,
and the return on the retained strip — minus, for the first time, **the cost of the retention
capital itself**. Gate 6 exists because that number has to be known before the phase starts.

**Next step:** an RT-6 companion (or a `--services` mode) covering Phases 1–2, with the same
`SOURCED` / `ASSUMED` discipline as the existing config. Logged as **M-41**. Until it exists, no
Phase 1 profitability claim should be made, in either direction.

## 8. Decision points — and why stopping is allowed

Track C has **three** decisions, not one. Treating it as a single irreversible commitment is the
main way people talk themselves out of it.

| | When | The question | If no |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **D0** | Now | Enter Phase 1? | Cheap and reversible. The cost of being wrong is a quarter of outreach and a counsel bill. |
| **D1** | Month ~12 | Do Lanes A and B clear the cash floor? | The model is wrong, not slow. `solo-operator-track.md` §9 names the test: if no originator will pay for a diagnostic, Lane A does not exist. Restructure or stop. |
| **D2** | Month ~30–36 | Convert to Phase 3, against the seven gates? | **Stay a practice.** See below. |

### Stopping at Phase 2 is a legitimate terminal state

A profitable design, standard-setting and verification practice — theories 7, 3 and 6 — is a real
business with recurring revenue, no balance sheet, and no key-person capital requirement. It is
not a failed attempt at being a structurer.

This has to be said explicitly, because if Phase 3 is the only definition of success then Phase 1
gets treated as a waiting room: under-priced, under-marketed, and staffed with whatever attention
is left over. That is the actual failure mode of Track C, and it is more likely than any of the
market risks.

The honest framing: **Phase 1 and 2 are the business. Phase 3 is an option that Phase 1 and 2 pay
for and keep open.** Gates 3 and 4 may close it on the evidence, and that is a result, not a loss.

## 9. Risks specific to Track C

These are in addition to the risks in `business-plan.md` §9 and `solo-operator-track.md` §9. They
exist *because* the plan is two-phase.

| Risk | Mitigation |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Phase 1 is treated as a waiting room** and under-invested | §8. Name Phase 2 as a terminal state; hold D2 as a real decision with the option of no. |
| **The conversion never gets made** because there is always one more engagement to run | D2 is calendared, not triggered by mood. Gates are checked on a date. |
| **Advisory revenue becomes the constraint on structuring**: the clients who pay Phase 1 fees are the originators whose books Phase 3 pools, and honest structuring may require telling a paying client its book is not investable | Separate the diagnostic fee from any structuring outcome — never contingent. Disclose the dual role in writing at engagement. This is a real conflict and it is managed by disclosure and fee structure, not by hoping it does not arise. |
| **Positioning whiplash** — the market reads a practice that describes itself as a future structurer as neither | Lead with the practice. Phase 3 is internal planning, not external messaging, until gate 1 is met. |
| **The seasoning clock is started late** because deal 1 was held out for size | §5.2. Small and early beats large and late. |
| **Key-person risk blocks Phase 3** — investors will not commit to a structure whose sponsor is one person | The published standard and open tooling (§5.4) make the method outlive the person; a first hire becomes unavoidable at Phase 3 and should be budgeted at gate 6, not discovered. |
