# Where To Play, and What the First Pilots Look Like

**Status:** Business scoping · **Owner:** BB · **Drafted:** 2026-09-09

**Scope correction.** The 2026-09-08 documents scoped this as a research programme wearing a
business hat — randomised protocol assignment, variance decomposition, seven conversion gates.
That was inherited from the PhD track and it is the wrong shape. This document scopes it as a
business: **where the opportunity is, who it reaches, and what a first pilot actually looks like.**

`solo-operator-track.md`, `track-c-plan.md` and `decision-register.md` stay in the repo — the
revenue-lane structure and the five blocking decisions in the register survive this correction

## 1. The screen — business criteria, not research criteria

Six questions, in order. A sector that fails 2 or 3 is out regardless of how it scores elsewhere.

| | Question | Why it decides |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **1** | Is there a financing gap with an **identified structural cause**? | "People need money" is not a gap. "Lenders cannot price this because the cash flow is undocumented" is. |
| **2** | **Is there an aggregator?** | An organisation that already holds the relationship and already handles money. Without one there is no distribution and no counterparty — and no business. |
| **3** | **Is the cash flow visible and assignable?** | If you cannot see the receivable, you cannot design a product around it. This is the hard filter. |
| **4** | Is capital already looking at this sector? | Determines whether anything you arrange can ever close. You want money waiting for a bankable structure, not money to be persuaded the sector exists. |
| **5** | Does a guarantee or first-loss facility already exist? | Someone else has to take the junior risk. If nobody does, deal one is unfinanceable. |
| **6** | Can one person operate here? | Language, travel, time zone, regulatory perimeter, and whether you have any way in at all. |

**The organising insight: follow the visible, contractible cash flow — not the neediest
population.** That is the difference between a structuring business and a charity, and it is the
single most useful filter here. It also honestly bounds the impact claim (§3).

## 2. Sector scan

Scored against the screen. **These are working judgements to be verified in the market, not
sourced findings** — the verification pass is §7.

| Sector | Gap | Aggregator | Cash flow visible | Capital looking | Guarantees exist | Solo-operable | Call |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Agri value chains with a formal off-taker** (coffee, cocoa, dairy, tea, horticulture) | Strong | **Strong** — co-ops, unions, processors, exporters | **Strong** — the off-taker pays for the crop, and that payment can be escrowed or assigned | Strong | Yes | Moderate | **Lead** |
| **PAYGo solar / productive-use asset finance** | Strong | **Strong** — the distributor is a company, not a diffuse group | **Strongest** — digitally metered repayment, best data quality available anywhere in this space | Very strong | Yes | Good | **Comparator, not entry** |
| **Migrant-worker and remittance-linked finance** | Real, under-served | Moderate — employers, recruiters, community orgs | Strong *if* the employer cooperates — wages are assignable | Thin | Few | **Good** (Israel proximity) | **Niche option** |
| **Urban microenterprise via business associations** | Strong | Moderate — associations, market committees | **Weak** — daily takings are not contractible | Moderate | Some | Good | Out on 3 |
| **Health / education fee finance** | Real | Moderate — schools, clinics | Moderate, but small and politically exposed | Thin | Few | Moderate | Out on 4 |
| **Housing and WASH microfinance** | Strong | Weak | **Weak** — long tenor, no assignable receivable | Thin | Few | Moderate | Out on 2 and 3 |

### Why agri value chains lead

Not because agriculture is the most deserving sector — because it is the only one where a poor
household's income arrives as **a documented payment from an identifiable counterparty**. The
cooperative or processor knows what each member delivered, at what grade, and what it paid them.
That record is the receivable. Everything the business does — the schema, the underwriting, the
monitoring, the facility — needs exactly that and nothing else provides it at this income level.

The existing coffee work (LIT-031, LIT-033, MEMO-9) already sits here, and LIT-033's finding that
cooperative member registers are obtainable through the union and the government cooperatives
agency is a distribution and verification asset, not just a research one.

### Why PAYGo is the comparator and not the entry point

It scores highest on the screen and it is the wrong first move. The sector has already done real
receivables transactions, which means specialist arrangers exist, standards have partly settled,
and a solo entrant is late. **Its value to you is as proof that the model works** — when a funder
asks whether community-originated receivables can be structured, PAYGo is the answer, and your
pitch is that the same logic has not yet been applied where the aggregator is a cooperative rather
than a company.

### The honest weakness in the lead sector

Agri cash flows are **seasonal and covariate**. One harvest a year, and a bad season hits every
member at once. LIT-031 records leaf rust across more than half of Central America's coffee area
in a single event. That is a genuine design constraint, not a footnote — it means the first
products should be **short-tenor and tied to a specific harvest**, and that any portfolio-level
product needs either a guarantee or geographic spread. It does not need to be resolved by research
before you can sell anything.

## 3. Population — who this reaches, and the ceiling

**Reached:** households with a productive activity and a documented relationship to an aggregator.
Cooperative members, contracted outgrowers, association members with a payment relationship,
PAYGo customers.

**Not reached, by design:** anyone outside those structures. The unorganised, the
newly-displaced, the poorest — people whose income leaves no record.

**This ceiling is set by the business model and it should be stated rather than blurred.** A
structuring business reaches people through the institutions that already serve them; it does not
create those institutions. That is a real limit on the impact claim and it is better said in the
first meeting than discovered by a funder later. The compensating argument is that the population
inside these structures is very large, chronically under-served relative to its documentation
quality, and is exactly the group where a financing gap is *structural* rather than a matter of
creditworthiness.

**Where the impact actually lands**, if the thesis holds: cheaper and better-timed credit for
members (pre-harvest rather than post-harvest at distress prices), and a cooperative that can
finance its own working capital instead of rationing it. Both are measurable without a trial (§6).

## 4. Geography

Geography follows the aggregator relationship you can actually obtain. Rank on the screen, then on
access.

| | Fit | Note |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **East Africa** (Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania, Rwanda, Ethiopia) | **Strongest** | Deepest cooperative infrastructure, most active agri-lending capital, several guarantee facilities operating, English-workable |
| **Central America** (Honduras, Guatemala, Nicaragua) | Strong | The coffee literature base sits here; Spanish; further to travel; the leaf-rust covariate risk is documented and severe |
| **West Africa** (Ghana, Côte d'Ivoire, Senegal) | Moderate | Cocoa aggregation is strong; more concentrated, more political, harder for a solo entrant |
| **Israel** | Weak for agri, **good for the migrant-worker niche** | Original justification in `business-plan.md` §7 was proximity for a research configuration. As a market for this business it is small; as a base of operations and for one niche product it is defensible |

**Recommendation: East Africa, agri value chains, one country.** Choose the country by where you
can get a first conversation, not by ranking — the difference between the top three is smaller
than the difference between having an introduction and not having one.

## 5. Three pilots

A pilot here is **the smallest real deployment that earns money and produces evidence** — not an
experiment. Each is specified by what money moves, what you earn, and what you learn.

### Pilot 1 — Portfolio readiness review

| | |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Partner** | One cooperative union or agri-lender |
| **Duration** | 6–8 weeks |
| **What you do** | Score their current origination and repayment records against the RT-1 field list; produce a gap report, a costed remediation plan, and a clean data pack of their existing portfolio |
| **What money moves** | None. A fee to you. |
| **You earn** | A fixed fee |
| **You learn** | Whether the data problem is as bad as the thesis says; what remediation actually costs; whether anyone will pay for this |
| **Cost to you** | Time plus one trip |
| **Proves** | That the diagnostic is a sellable product — the whole cash floor of the business |

This replaces the previous EXP-22 framing. Same underlying work, without asking a first-time
client to host a randomised trial with timestamped logs and HR sign-off for time-and-motion study.

### Pilot 2 — A guaranteed pre-harvest credit facility

| | |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Partner** | One cooperative union (borrower/on-lender), one lender, one guarantee provider |
| **Size** | Low single-digit millions USD |
| **Duration** | 6–12 months to close, one season to run |
| **What you do** | Design the product and the facility: ticket size, timing against the harvest calendar, repayment deducted at delivery, the guarantee structure, and the reporting pack the lender needs |
| **What money moves** | Real capital to real members |
| **You earn** | Design fee plus arranging fee |
| **You learn** | Whether the structure closes, what lenders actually require, what the guarantee costs |
| **Proves** | The core business — that you can assemble investor, aggregator and risk cover into a working facility |

**Repayment deducted at delivery is the mechanism that makes this work.** The cooperative already
pays members for the crop; the loan is netted against that payment. The receivable and the
collection are the same event, which is why this sector clears the screen and the others do not.

### Pilot 3 — Bundled credit with a shock trigger

| | |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Partner** | Pilot 2's cooperative and lender, plus a licensed insurer or parametric underwriter |
| **Duration** | Follows pilot 2 by a season |
| **What you do** | Add a climate or price trigger that flexes repayment when the harvest fails, so the loan does not compound the shock |
| **What money moves** | Premium and, if triggered, a payout |
| **You earn** | Design fee; possibly ongoing product-management fee |
| **You learn** | Whether the bundle improves lender appetite enough to price better |
| **Proves** | That the design layer adds value beyond arranging |

**Sequence pilots 1 → 2 → 3, and only start pilot 3 if pilot 2 closes.** Pilot 3 needs a risk
carrier, which is a whole additional partner class and sales cycle. Note that MEMO-5 already found
compulsory bundling suppressed demand in two prior attempts — so if pilot 3 runs, the cover is
optional or embedded in pricing, not mandatory.

## 6. Measuring impact without a trial

No RCTs. What a commercial operation can credibly report:

1. **Portfolio outcomes from the facility's own data** — disbursement timing against the harvest
   calendar, repayment rates, arrears, renewal rates. RT-3 already does this. This is operational
   reporting and it is what a lender wants anyway.
2. **Pre/post on a small set of member indicators**, collected through the cooperative's existing
   member records rather than a survey operation: price received per unit, share of crop sold at
   harvest versus later, borrowing from other sources.
3. **Benchmarked against published effect sizes** from the literature already in the matrix, so
   the claim is "our outcomes are consistent with / better than the range reported in X" rather
   than an attribution claim of our own.
4. **A light third-party review** — a local university or an evaluation consultancy reading the
   monitoring data and writing two pages. Cheap, and it is what makes the numbers usable by a
   funder.

**Report outcomes, do not claim attribution.** Without a counterfactual you cannot say the product
caused the change, and saying so is the fastest way to lose credibility with exactly the funders
who matter. Stating "here is what happened, measured honestly, and here is what the literature
says similar products achieve" is both defensible and sufficient for commercial purposes. If a
research partner later wants to run a proper evaluation on top of a live facility, that is their
grant and their design — not this business's cost.

## 7. What to verify next

Two weeks of desk work, then conversations. Not a research programme:

1. **Confirm the guarantee and incentive facilities** operating in the chosen country for agri
   lending — who they are, what they cover, what they require. Aceli Africa (PT-14) is the first
   call.
2. **Name 8–10 candidate aggregators** — cooperative unions and agri-lenders in one country, with
   scale and contact route.
3. **Name 3–5 lenders** already active in agri portfolio lending there (PT-16 and comparables).
4. **Check the regulatory perimeter** for advisory and arranging fees in that country (OQ-20).
5. **Test the diagnostic offer** on three of the aggregators from (2). That conversation answers
   more than another month of desk analysis.
