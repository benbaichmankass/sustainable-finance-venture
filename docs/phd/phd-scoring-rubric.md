# PhD Program Scoring Rubric

*How the 43 candidate programs in data/phd-programs.csv are scored and ranked for outreach. Companion to the interactive workbook (PhD-Program-Tracker.xlsx, in the project) and the landscape synthesis (phd-landscape.md). This is a decision aid, not a verdict: the scores encode the current research, and every program flagged "to verify" in Verification\_Status should be confirmed by direct email before its ranking hardens.*

## The problem the rubric solves

No single department combines the four pillars this project needs (development economics and impact evaluation; financial inclusion and microinsurance; securitization and structured finance; sustainable and blended finance). So the rubric does not look for one perfect programme. It scores each candidate on six factors, weights them toward what matters most for this project and this applicant, and groups the field so the first wave of supervisor outreach goes to the strongest combined fits rather than the single highest-prestige name.

## The six criteria and their scales

Each program is scored 1 to 5 on each criterion. The anchors below define what a score means so the numbers stay comparable across programs.

**1\. Supervisor fit** — a named academic actively publishing on the thesis topics (microfinance, financial inclusion, impact evaluation, microinsurance, securitization/structured finance). 5 \= one or more verified, directly on-topic supervisors. 4 \= a verified supervisor covering a core pillar strongly, with a caveat (e.g. an RCT/impact-evaluation lead, or a microfinance scholar whose current capacity needs confirming). 3 \= a plausible or likely fit but no confirmed on-topic supervisor, or a verified but tangential match. 2 \= weak. 1 \= none identifiable.

**2\. Venture and pilot fit** — part-time or fieldwork flexibility, tolerance of running the venture in parallel, and proximity to the Israel and Africa pilot sites. 5 \= an explicit part-time or venture-parallel route, or a "the venture is the thesis" alignment, plus pilot proximity. 3 \= full-time but fieldwork-friendly and RCT-native. 1 \= full-time residential, relocation required, venture-incompatible.

**3\. Funding and cost** — stipend, fee coverage, cost of living, and the likelihood of actually securing an award. 5 \= a salaried or guaranteed-funded post with low or no fees. 3 \= a competitive scholarship. 1 \= self-funded or high total cost.

**4\. Method / impact-evaluation fit** — strength in RCT and quasi-experimental impact evaluation, the backbone of the verification-heavy design. 5 \= a leading RCT / field-experiment group. 3 \= solid econometrics. 1 \= none.

**5\. Structured-finance fit** — depth in securitization, tranching, first-loss design, and actuarial / microinsurance pricing. 5 \= specialist capital-markets or actuarial faculty. 3 \= general finance. 1 \= none.

**6\. Prestige and network** — reputation, DFI and industry links, placement, and credibility. 5 \= a world-leading brand with strong development-finance ties. 1 \= limited reach.

## Weights

| Criterion | Weight |
| :---- | :---- |
| Supervisor fit | 25% |
| Venture and pilot fit | 25% |
| Funding and cost | 20% |
| Method / impact-evaluation fit | 10% |
| Structured-finance fit | 10% |
| Prestige and network | 10% |

The three factors flagged as priorities — supervisor fit, venture/pilot fit, and funding — carry 70% of the weight between them. Method and structured-finance fit sit at 10% each because, given that no department has everything, those gaps are expected to be filled by a co-supervisor rather than the primary programme, so they inform the choice without dominating it. Prestige is a deliberate 10%: a tie-breaker, not a driver. The weights are the single most important lever; changing them in the workbook re-ranks the field instantly, and it is worth testing (for example, raising venture/pilot fit if staying in Israel becomes non-negotiable, or funding if self-funding is ruled out).

## The uncertainty rule

A program whose on-topic supervisor is not yet confirmed — anything marked "supervisor to verify / to identify / to secure" in Verification\_Status — is capped at 3 out of 5 on Supervisor fit, however promising the department looks. This keeps unproven fits from topping the ranking on the strength of a name we have not yet confirmed can and will supervise. In this pass the cap only moved one program (BRAC/SOAS, from 4 to 3); every other capped program was already at 3 or below, which is a sign the base scores were already honest about where the evidence is thin.

## Tiering

Programs are grouped by weighted score so the ranking drives action rather than just sorting names:

- **Priority** (score at or above 3.55) — the first wave of supervisor outreach.  
- **Consider** (2.95 to 3.54) — strong on some axes with a specific caveat; a good second wave, or a first wave if a Priority program's supervisor declines.  
- **Backup** (below 2.95) — pursue only if a specific supervisor or funding route opens up.

## How to read this

The Priority group is deliberately mixed by region and format so the first outreach wave is not a bet on one geography: it spans a funded RCT-and-insurance fit in the UK (UEA), the two best European microfinance homes (CERMi, IHEID), the strongest Israel option that lets the venture run (HUJI Business School), a part-time-friendly Asian route (Krea), and several salaried-PhD European programmes (Groningen, KU Leuven, Wageningen). Two structural notes carry over from the landscape synthesis: most of these are development-side homes that will need a finance or actuarial co-supervisor for the securitization craft, and the ranking rewards confirmed supervisors, so a "Consider" program can jump into Priority the moment a named supervisor there says yes. Re-score in the workbook as replies come in.

## Caveats

The scores are the author's best reading of the research as of August 2026, not measured values. Stipend figures, deadlines, part-time eligibility and individual supervisors' current capacity all shift year to year and several are still unverified (see Verification\_Status in the tracker). Treat the ranking as a prioritised worklist for confirmation and outreach, not a final answer.
